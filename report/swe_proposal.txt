Executive Summary

The CUET Online Stationery System is a web-based platform developed to provide CUET
students with convenient access to stationery and academic supplies within the university
campus. The system reduces the need for off-campus purchases by allowing students to
browse products, place orders, and receive items efficiently through a centralized online
service.

    The platform offers essential features such as a categorized product catalog, search and
filter options, shopping cart and checkout facilities, multiple payment methods, and real-
time order tracking. An administrative dashboard enables vendors and administrators to
manage inventory, process orders, and monitor deliveries effectively. Role-based access
control ensures secure and appropriate system usage for students and vendors.

    The system follows a client-server architectural model with a centralized database to
maintain data consistency and scalability. It is implemented using Next.js and Mon-
goDB, while JIRA is used for project planning, task management, and team coordination
throughout the development process.

    By automating order handling and inventory management, the CUET Online Sta-
tionery System improves operational efficiency and supports CUET's vision of a smart,
student-oriented campus. Future enhancements include mobile application development
and extended automation features to further improve system usability and performance.

1 Introduction

In today's fast-paced academic environment, students often face challenges in access-
ing essential study materials conveniently and efficiently. CUET students, in particular,
frequently rely on nearby stationery shops or make off-campus trips to purchase pens,
notebooks, lab materials, and other academic supplies. Such trips can be time-consuming
and inconvenient, especially during exam periods, late-night study sessions, or unfavorable
weather conditions.

    To address these challenges, the CUET Online Stationery platform has been pro-
posed as a comprehensive digital solution. This system allows students to browse a wide
range of stationery items, place orders, and receive their supplies directly at campus halls
or designated pickup points. By integrating features such as an intuitive product catalog,
easy payment options through bKash or Cash on Delivery (COD), order tracking, and an
AI-driven recommendation system, the platform aims to streamline the entire process of
acquiring academic materials.

1.1 Goals and Objectives of the Project

The main goal of the CUET Online Stationery system is to provide CUET students
with a convenient and time-saving digital platform for purchasing stationery and academic
supplies directly from campus. The project aims to enhance accessibility and promote a
smart campus environment.

Objectives
   · To provide a time-saving solution for purchasing stationery within CUET.
   · To ensure students have quick access to essential academic supplies at all times.
   · To simplify order management, inventory control, and delivery processes for the
       admin.
   · To implement AI-driven item recommendations based on student needs.
   · To offer secure payment options through bKash or Cash on Delivery.

1.2 Scope of the Work

The project focuses on developing an online platform where CUET students can order
stationery items such as pens, notebooks, lab materials, and art supplies. Orders will be
processed and delivered to campus halls or designated pickup points. The system supports
both student-facing and admin-facing interfaces for smooth operation and management.

1.2.1 Current Situation and Context
Currently, CUET students depend on nearby stationery shops or off-campus trips to
purchase essential items. This process becomes inconvenient during exam periods, late-
night study sessions, or unfavorable weather conditions. The proposed system addresses
these issues by creating a campus-centric e-commerce platform that allows students to
browse, order, and receive stationery efficiently within the university premises. This aligns
with CUET's mission to build a technologically advanced and student-friendly learning
environment.

2.2 Process Model Used

The project follows a Prototyping Process Model integrated with Agile principles. This
hybrid approach is effective for projects where requirements evolve through user inter-
action and continuous feedback. In this model, an initial prototype of the system is
developed to demonstrate major functionalities. The prototype is evaluated by users and
stakeholders to gather feedback, which is used to refine the system through successive
iterations until the final version is achieved.

2.2.1 Rationale for Choosing the Process Model

The Agile-based Prototyping Model was selected for the CUET Online Stationery project
because it aligns with the project's objectives, team size, and academic timeline. The
following points highlight the key reasons behind this selection:

                                                       11
   · User-Centered and Iterative Design: The project emphasizes a user-friendly
       web interface. Early prototypes allow usability testing and feedback, ensuring the
       design remains intuitive and aligned with CUET students' expectations.

   · Early Feedback and Requirement Refinement: Each prototype iteration in-
       volves collecting input from students and stakeholders. This feedback-driven cycle
       refines requirements and interface design, minimizing rework and improving accu-
       racy.

   · Flexibility and Adaptability: Integrating Agile principles allows the team to
       respond quickly to changing requirements or new feature ideas, such as AI-based
       recommendations or coupon systems, without disrupting progress.

   · Reduced Development Risk: Early evaluation of functional prototypes helps
       detect usability or technical issues before full-scale implementation, reducing risks
       and improving overall quality.

   · Efficient Use of Time and Resources: Given the short academic timeline and
       limited resources, this model enables rapid prototyping and testing of key modules
       without excessive documentation or lengthy planning phases.

   · Enhanced Team Collaboration: The Agile structure encourages regular commu-
       nication and coordination, allowing different modules (product catalog, cart system,
       admin dashboard) to be developed and tested concurrently.

   · Higher User Acceptance: Continuous stakeholder involvement during prototype
       evaluation ensures the final system closely reflects user needs, resulting in greater
       satisfaction and adoption among CUET students.

2.4 Constraints to Project Implementation

Several potential constraints may affect the implementation of the CUET Online Sta-
tionery project. These include:

   · Technical Limitations: As the development relies on student-level resources and
       free-tier services, limited server capacity or data storage may restrict scalability.

   · Time Constraints: The project must be completed within the academic semester,
       leaving a short time for testing and optimization.

   · Financial Constraints: The team operates under a minimal budget, relying pri-
       marily on free or educational software licenses, which may limit access to premium
       tools or APIs.

   · Connectivity Issues: Since the system depends on stable internet connectivity,
       network interruptions within the campus may affect ordering operations.

   · Human Resource Constraints: The project is being developed by a small stu-
       dent team, which may slow down parallel progress in different modules such as
       frontend, backend, and testing.

3 Requirement Specifications

3.1 Stakeholders of the System

The stakeholders of the CUET Online Stationery system include all individuals and
entities who are directly or indirectly involved in its development, operation, or usage.
The primary stakeholders are described as follows:

   · Students: The main end-users of the system. They can browse, order, and purchase
       stationery items through the platform. They are the primary beneficiaries of the
       convenience, accessibility, and time-saving features offered by the system.

   · Admins/Vendors: Responsible for managing the platform's operations. Their
       duties include updating the product catalog, monitoring inventory levels, processing
       orders, managing deliveries, and handling customer feedback.

   · System Developers: The project team responsible for designing, developing, test-
       ing, and maintaining the platform. They ensure that the system meets all functional
       and non-functional requirements while maintaining security and performance stan-
       dards.

    Collectively, these stakeholders contribute to the system's development, operation,
and sustainability, ensuring that it serves the CUET community effectively and reliably.

4 Architectural Design

4.1 Architectural Model/Style Used

For the CUET Online Stationery System, a data-centered architectural model has
been adopted with a primary focus on centralized data management, consistency, and
reliable information sharing among system components.

    In this design, a centralized data repository acts as the core of the system. All
major system components--including customers, vendors, and administrators--interact
indirectly through this shared data store. The repository maintains product catalogs,
user credentials, order details, payment records, and delivery information, ensuring that
all system operations are based on a single, consistent source of data.

    The client-side modules (Customer Module and Vendor Module) access and up-
date information by communicating with the centralized database via the system services.
Customers can browse products, place orders, and complete payments, while vendors up-
date inventory levels, manage product listings, and confirm order fulfillment. Each action
performed by any component is reflected immediately in the shared data repository, main-
taining real-time synchronization across the system.

    This data-centered architecture improves data integrity, system reliability, and ease of
maintenance, making it well suited for continuous and coordinated operation within the
CUET campus environment.

The selection of a data-centered architectural model for the CUET Online Stationery

ensuring efficient service delivery for CUET students and vendors.

API, cron jobs help automate routine tasks that ensure the system remains updated and
efficient.

    Cron-based automation includes:
   · Inventory Updates: Automatically update stock levels based on orders and re-

       stocking events.
   · Email Notifications: Send automated reminders for order confirmations, product

       promotions, or back-in-stock alerts.
   · Sales Report Generation: Generate daily, weekly, or monthly sales summaries

       for administrative review.
   · System Maintenance: Clean up outdated logs or expired data to maintain system

       performance.

                                                       42
    Cron-based scheduling ensures timely execution of critical background tasks with min-
imal overhead, helping the system maintain smooth operations without manual interven-
tion.
5.3.2 Queue System (Asynchronous Processing)
Task queues are used to manage time-consuming processes by running them asynchronously
in the background. These queues allow tasks such as payment processing, product feed-
back analysis, or report generation to be handled without interrupting the main applica-
tion or affecting the user experience.

    Queues are implemented to handle tasks efficiently:
   · Order Processing: After an order is placed, tasks like updating stock, processing

       payments, and generating invoices are queued for background processing.
   · Product Feedback: When feedback is submitted, it is placed in a queue for

       analysis and categorization without delaying the user's interaction.
   · Large Report Generation: Generate sales or inventory reports in the back-

       ground, ensuring real-time interactions with the application remain unaffected.
    Using a queue system improves the system's scalability by distributing heavy work-
loads across background workers, ensuring the application remains fast, responsive, and
efficient even during high usage periods.

5.7 Database Design Model and Script

The database is implemented using MongoDB, a NoSQL document-oriented database,
integrated with Next.js full-stack framework. Mongoose ODM (Object Data Modeling)
library is used to define schemas and manage data validation. This combination provides
flexibility, scalability, and seamless integration with the JavaScript ecosystem.

5.7.1 Collection Descriptions
Students Collection The students collection stores information about registered stu-
dents who can place orders through the system. Each student document contains authen-
tication credentials and contact information.

   · Primary Identifier: _id (ObjectId, auto-generated)
   · Unique Fields: student_id, email
   · Attributes: student ID, name, email, phone, password (hashed), hall name
   · References: Referenced by orders, payments, and feedbacks collections

Admins Collection The admins collection maintains administrative user accounts with
elevated privileges for system management and oversight.

   · Primary Identifier: _id (ObjectId, auto-generated)
   · Unique Fields: username, email
   · Attributes: username, email, password (hashed)

                                                       59
Inventory Collection The inventory collection catalogs all products available for
purchase, including detailed product information and stock levels.

   · Primary Identifier: _id (ObjectId, auto-generated)
   · Attributes: product name, category, description, brand, price, stock quantity
   · References: Referenced by order items and feedbacks collections

Orders Collection The orders collection records all purchase orders placed by stu-
dents, tracking order amounts, status, and delivery information.

   · Primary Identifier: _id (ObjectId, auto-generated)
   · Foreign Reference: student_id references students collection
   · Attributes: student ID, total amount, order status, delivery address, order times-

       tamp, delivery timestamp
   · References: Referenced by order items, payments, and feedbacks collections

Order Items Collection The order_items collection stores line-item details for each
order, linking orders with inventory items.

   · Primary Identifier: _id (ObjectId, auto-generated)
   · Foreign References:

          ­ order_id references orders collection
          ­ inventory_id references inventory collection
   · Attributes: order ID, inventory ID, quantity, unit price

Payments Collection The payments collection tracks all payment transactions asso-
ciated with orders, supporting multiple payment methods and statuses.

   · Primary Identifier: _id (ObjectId, auto-generated)
   · Foreign References:

          ­ student_id references students collection
          ­ order_id references orders collection
   · Attributes: student ID, order ID, payment method, payment status, amount,
       payment timestamp

                                                       60
Feedbacks Collection The feedbacks collection stores customer reviews and ratings
for products, enabling quality monitoring and customer satisfaction tracking.

   · Primary Identifier: _id (ObjectId, auto-generated)
   · Foreign References:

          ­ student_id references students collection
          ­ inventory_id references inventory collection
          ­ order_id references orders collection
   · Attributes: student ID, inventory ID, order ID, rating (integer), comment (text)

6.3 Techniques Used for Test Generation

In this project, both white box testing and black box testing techniques were applied to
generate effective test cases for the CUET Online Stationery System. These techniques
were selected to ensure comprehensive validation of both internal logic and external func-
tionality of the system.

    White Box Testing Technique: Basis Path Testing
The Basis Path Testing technique was used to analyze the internal control flow of critical
modules such as authentication, order processing, and inventory management. This tech-
nique involves constructing flow graphs of the program logic and identifying independent
execution paths. Test cases were derived to ensure that each statement and decision point
in the code was executed at least once, thereby improving code coverage and reducing the
possibility of logical errors.

    Black Box Testing Technique: Equivalence Partitioning
Equivalence Partitioning was employed as the primary black box testing technique to

                                                       73
evaluate system functionality based on input conditions. Input data was divided into
valid and invalid equivalence classes, such as correct and incorrect login credentials or
valid and invalid product quantities. Test cases were designed from these partitions to
verify that the system responds correctly to both acceptable and erroneous inputs without
examining the internal code structure.

6.4 Assessment of the Goodness of the Test Suite

The testing phase of the CUET Online Stationery System followed a systematic and
comprehensive approach to ensure software correctness, reliability, and usability. Both
white box and black box testing methods were applied to cover different aspects of system
validation.

    White box testing using the Basis Path Testing technique enabled analysis of the
logical complexity of internal modules and ensured adequate execution path coverage.
This was particularly important due to the interaction between multiple components
such as user authentication, product management, and order processing.

    In addition, black box testing using Equivalence Partitioning focused on validating
functional requirements from the user's perspective. By testing valid and invalid input
conditions across various features, the test suite ensured that the system behaves as
expected under different scenarios. The combined application of these testing techniques
enhanced the robustness of the test suite and increased confidence in the system's overall
reliability and performance.

6.5 Sustainability Plan

The sustainability of the CUET Online Stationery System is ensured through design
choices that support long-term usability, maintainability, and growth. The sustainability
plan emphasizes balanced development, focusing on system efficiency, ease of maintenance,
and adaptability to future requirements. Rather than prioritizing rapid expansion, the
system is designed to evolve steadily while maintaining stability and performance.

6.5.1 Scalability

Scalability is a key consideration in the design of the CUET Online Stationery System.
The system follows a client-server architectural model with centralized data management,
enabling it to support an increasing number of users without significant performance

                                                       74
degradation. The use of modular components and a centralized database allows the sys-
tem to handle growing workloads, expanded product catalogs, and increased transaction
volumes efficiently. This ensures that the platform remains responsive and reliable as user
demand increases over time.
6.5.2 Flexibility and Customization
The system is designed with flexibility and ease of customization in mind. The modular
structure of the web application allows individual components, such as product manage-
ment or order tracking, to be modified or extended without affecting the entire system.
This design approach enables administrators to adapt the system to changing user needs,
introduce new features, or update existing functionalities with minimal effort. As a result,
the CUET Online Stationery System remains adaptable and cost-effective throughout its
lifecycle.
