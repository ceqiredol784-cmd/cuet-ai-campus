Executive Summary

The CUET Store System is an automated inventory management solution devel-
oped for Chittagong University of Engineering and Technology (CUET). It aims
to streamline resource management, enhance transparency, and improve the effi-
ciency of resource allocation across departments by centralizing and automating
inventory processes.
The system enables real-time tracking of resources, including projectors, furniture,
and academic tools. It supports key functionalities such as inventory management,
order request submissions, approval workflows, and report generation, all designed
to meet CUET's specific needs. Role-based access ensures that users like CUET
Authorities, Department Heads, and Admins can securely interact with the system
according to their responsibilities.
Built using a Layered Architecture, the CUET Store System ensures scalabil-
ity, security, and maintainability, while technologies like React.js, Node.js, and
MongoDB provide a robust foundation for long-term performance. The system
improves efficiency by eliminating delays and errors associated with manual pro-
cesses.
Future enhancements include mobile app integration for remote access and AI-
based inventory forecasting to optimize resource planning. By automating work-
flows and centralizing management, the CUET Store System fosters improved
decision-making and operational excellence at CUET, ensuring it meets the uni-
versity's growing needs.

1 Introduction

The CUET Store system is a centralized inventory management solu-
tion designed to optimize resource tracking, distribution and acqui-
sition across Chittagong University of Engineering and Technology
(CUET). By automating resource management processes, it ensures
transparency, efficiency and accountability in inventory operations.
The system aims to optimize resource allocation and simplify op-
erations for CUET departments, making inventory handling more
effective and reliable.

1.1 Goals and Objectives of the Project

The main objective of the CUET Store system is to provide CUET's
departments and administrative divisions with an automated inven-
tory management platform. The project's objectives are to:

  · Simplify Resource Management: Keep an eye on and effec-
      tively manage inventory.

  · Facilitate Order Workflows: Offer a smooth procedure for
      departmental requests and approvals.

  · Boost Responsibility: Record every permission and transac-
      tion for auditing purposes.

  · Make Reporting and Analytics Possible: Produce insights
      to improve inventory management and utilization.

1.2 Scope of the Work

Automation and optimization of the inventory management process
across CUET are the goals of the CUET Store system. It will keep
track of and oversee a variety of resources to guarantee effective de-
partmental distribution. The complete workflow, including order
requests, approval procedures, delivery confirmations and inventory

management, will be managed by the system. Additionally, it has
reporting tools for tracking order patterns and stock levels, which
helps in decision-making. The complete workflow, including order
requests, approval procedures, delivery confirmations and inventory
management, will be managed by the Store system.

1.2.1 Current Situation and Context

At the moment, CUET manages its inventory and resources either
manually or through disjointed systems, which results in:

  · Inefficiencies: Having trouble monitoring order statuses and
      inventory levels.

  · Delays: The manual procedure for approval makes it take
      longer to allocate resources.

  · Errors: The likelihood of inconsistencies is increased when
      there is no centralized database.

 By offering a unified digital platform customized to meet CUET's
unique needs, the CUET Store system tackles these issues.

1.2.2 Competing Products (Available in Market)

Several inventory management systems, such as Tally ERP and Zoho
Inventory, are available. However, they are not tailored for educa-
tional institutions. Some significant distinctions are:

  · Zoho Inventory: A powerful inventory tool, however it isn't
      appropriate for workflows with several roles, such as CUET's
      (e.g., department heads, CUET Authority).

  · Tally ERP: Provides accounting and inventory management,
      but is more complicated and difficult for CUET's varied stake-
      holders to utilize.

1.3 System Overview

An inventory management system called the CUET Store was cre-
ated to satisfy the administrative and academic requirements of CUET.
It consists of:

  · User authentication: CUET Authority, Department Heads
      and Administrators have role-based access.

  · Inventory management: Includes tools for adding, editing
      and managing items in addition to real-time stock level tracking.

  · Order management: A dynamic process that enables the
      Store Team to fulfill orders, CUET Authority to approve or
      reject requests and Department Heads to submit requests.

  · Analytics and Reporting: Detailed reporting capabilities for
      monitoring inventory consumption and identifying patterns.

  · Automated Notifications: Notifies interested parties of stock
      changes and order statuses.

2.2 Process Model Used

The Spiral Model, combined with Prototyping, has been chosen for
developing the CUET Store Management System because of its iter-
ative and adaptable nature. This hybrid method merges the struc-
tured risk management and incremental development of the Spiral
Model with the quick feedback and visualization advantages of pro-
totyping. Each iteration consists of a cycle that includes planning,
risk assessment, development, and user feedback, allowing the team
to effectively adjust to changing requirements. Prototyping is essen-
tial in helping stakeholders visualize the system at different stages,
ensuring their feedback is integrated early and consistently.

                                 Figure 1: Evolutionary Model

This approach not only reduces risks but also aligns the final product
closely with user needs and expectations. By fostering continuous
improvement, stakeholder involvement, and efficient resource use,

                                                   12
this strategy guarantees that the CUET Store Management System
is both strong and responsive to the organization's evolving needs.

2.2.1 Rationale for Choosing the Spiral Model

Flexibility and Evolving Requirements:

The CUET Store involves diverse stakeholders, including department
heads and CUET authorities, each with unique needs. The Spi-
ral Model's iterative approach allows for continuous refinement and
adaptation based on feedback, ensuring that the system evolves in
line with these needs.
Risk Management:
By breaking the project into smaller phases, the Spiral Model helps
identify and address potential issues early in the process. This in-
cremental development ensures that risks, whether technical or func-
tional, are mitigated before they become significant obstacles.
Saves Time and Budget:
Early risk detection and iterative improvements reduce the likelihood
of major changes late in development, saving time and money.
User Feedback:
Continuous interaction with users is a key feature of the model, en-
suring that the final product aligns with the specific needs of CUET
stakeholders.

2.3 Constraints to Project Implementation

Schedule Constraints
The iterative cycles of the Spiral Model can lead to extended de-
velopment timelines, as each phase involves detailed risk assessment,
planning, and refinement. Managing these cycles efficiently is crucial
to avoid delays.

                                                   13
Budget Constraints
While the Spiral Model helps manage risks and reduce costly rework,
the iterative approach requires continuous development, testing, and
stakeholder involvement, which can lead to increased expenses if not
carefully controlled.
Software Constraints
The ongoing iterative development may cause compatibility and func-
tionality challenges as new features are integrated. Regular test-
ing and refinement are necessary to ensure smooth operation, which
might delay the final delivery.
Hardware Constraints
The CUET Store Management System's performance is closely tied
to the capabilities of its hardware. Limited processing power or in-
sufficient server capacity may hinder system responsiveness, poten-
tially causing delays. Effective planning and resource management
are essential to overcome these constraints and ensure smooth system
implementation.

3.3 Development of SRS

Software Requirements Specification (SRS) for CUET Store
System

Introduction

Purpose
The purpose of this document is to define the requirements for the
CUET Store system. This system is designed to manage inven-
tory, streamline order management, and generate reports for resource
management within CUET.
Scope
The CUET Store system will be a centralized platform for managing
resources like projectors, chairs, and other items. It will support:

  · Inventory Management

  · Order Management

  · User Authentication and Role-Based Access

  · Reporting and Analytics
   The system will ensure efficient, transparent, and secure handling
of resources.

Overall Description

Product Perspective

The CUET Store system integrates with existing organizational work-
flows to provide a seamless resource management experience. It re-
lies on a centralized database to store data on inventory, orders, and
users.
User Classes and Characteristics

  · Admin: Manages inventory and generates reports.

                                                   23
  · Department Head: Submits order requests and tracks their
     status.

  · CUET Authority: Reviews and approves/rejects order re-
     quests.

  · Store Team: Processes and delivers approved orders.

Operating Environment
  · Web-based system accessible via modern browsers.
  · Built using the MERN stack (MongoDB, Express.js, React.js,
     Node.js).
  · Compatible with standard web technologies (HTML5, CSS, JavaScript).
  · Database: MongoDB, hosted locally or on cloud platforms like
     MongoDB Atlas.

Functional Requirements

User Authentication
  · Users must log in using email and password.
  · The system shall enforce secure password policies.
  · Role-based access control to ensure task-specific access.

Inventory Management
  · Admin can add, edit, and delete inventory items.
  · Inventory must track item details (name, price, quantity, cate-
     gory).
  · System to notify when stock levels are low.

                                                   24
Order Management
  · Department Heads can submit order requests.
  · CUET Authority can review and approve/reject orders.
  · Store Team updates order status (pending, approved, delivered).

Reporting and Analytics
  · Admin can generate inventory and order reports.
  · Analytics to display stock trends and order fulfillment rates.

Non-Functional Requirements

Performance
  · System should support up to 100 concurrent users.
  · Response time for major actions should be under 3 seconds.

Security
  · All sensitive data must be encrypted.
  · System must support HTTPS for all communications.

Usability
  · User interface should be intuitive and responsive.
  · Clear error messages for user actions.

Constraints

  · The system must use React.js for the frontend, Node.js for the
     backend and MongoDB for the database.

  · System must be deployable on a OS environment.

                                                   25
Assumptions and Dependencies

  · Assumes all users have internet access and a compatible browser.
  · Relies on accurate initial data input for inventory and user ac-

      counts.

Future Enhancements

  · Integration with mobile apps for remote access.
  · Predictive analytics for inventory forecasting.
  · Automated order reminders for pending approvals.

Conclusion

This SRS defines the functional and non-functional requirements for
the CUET Store system, ensuring it meets the needs of CUET's
resource management processes. Successful implementation will im-
prove efficiency, transparency, and traceability in managing resources.

3.5 Activity Diagram

An activity diagram is a type of UML (Unified Modeling Language)
diagram that represents the flow of activities or processes within a

4 Architecture

Architecture is a representation which enables a software engineer:
  · Analyze the effectiveness of design
  · Consider architectural alternatives
  · Reduce the risks

4.1 Architectural model/style used
Layered Architecture for CUET Store System

For the CUET Store System, we are utilizing the Layered Archi-
tecture. By dividing the system into discrete layers, each in charge
of carrying out particular functions, the Layered Architecture guar-
antees modularity, scalability, and separation of concerns. An illus-
tration of the broad layered architecture can be found below:

                                Figure 9: Layered Architecture
                                                   37
1. User Interface Layer:
This layer provides the interface for communicating with adminis-
trators, department heads, and the CUET Authority.

  · Ensures a smooth and intuitive experience for inventory man-
      agement, request submission, and data tracking.

2. Application Layer:
This layer puts business logic for order processing, inventory control,
and report creation into practice.

  · Serves as middleware, interacting with the lower layers and han-
      dling requests from the user interface layer.

3. Utility Layer:
This layer is in charge of managing APIs, facilitating database com-
munication, and managing secure data retrieval.

  · Acts as a bridge connecting the main database layer and the
      application layer.

4. Database/Core Layer:
This serves as the cornerstone of the architecture, housing all user,
inventory, and order data in one centralized location.

  · Ensures the system's overall security, accessibility, and data in-
      tegrity.

                                                   38
               Figure 10: Layered Architecture for CUET Store System

The CUET Store's functional requirements are met by this architec-
tural design, which guarantees a reliable, scalable, and maintained
system.

4.2 Rationale for Choosing Layered Architecture

  1. Modularity:
      The system's layered structure divides it into manageable com-
      ponents, facilitating better understanding, development, and
      maintenance.

  2. Separation of Concerns:
      Assigning distinct responsibilities to each layer ensures better
      maintainability and reduces overlap in functionalities.

  3. Scalability:
      Adding new features to specific layers, such as introducing ana-

                                                   39
     lytics in the Application Layer, can be achieved without affect-
     ing the entire system.

  4. Flexibility:
     Changes in one layer (e.g., updating the User Interface frame-
     work) do not impact other layers, ensuring system flexibility.

  5. Reusability:
     Layers like the Data Access Layer can be reused across different
     parts of the system, minimizing duplication of effort.

  6. Simplicity of Testing:
     The ability to test each layer individually improves the system's
     quality, reliability, and maintainability.
